# Xbox Series S: How to Pay Less, Which Model to Get, and How Cashback Cuts the Real Price

People searching "xbox series s" are usually after one of three things: what it costs today, whether it's still a sensible buy, or where the cheapest legitimate route in is. The first answer has moved a lot — mostly upward. The second has a real debate attached. The third is where most buyers leave money on the table.

## What the Xbox Series S Costs Now

Microsoft has raised recommended retail pricing on the Series S three times in recent memory. The console launched in November 2020 at $299.99. It moved to $380 in May 2025, then to $399.99 on 3 October 2025. By August 2026, GameSpot was reporting the 512GB model's retail price at $500. The 1TB version followed the same ladder, going from $429.99 to $449.99 in the October 2025 round.

| Timing | 512GB model | 1TB model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Launch (Nov 2020) | $299.99 | Not yet available |
| May 2025 increase | $379.99 | $429.99 |
| October 2025 increase | $399.99 | $449.99 |
| Reported August 2026 | $500 | — |

Those are US figures. The same increases landed globally, and T3 noted the UK absorbed roughly a £50 jump on the Series S in the earlier round.

Retail reality is messier than list price, though. Retailers have repeatedly undercut Microsoft's new recommended pricing. Kotaku flagged the 512GB model at $329 on Amazon right after the October 2025 increase — $71 below list. T3 caught it at $279.99 during the May 2025 hike. Costco stopped stocking Xbox hardware outright, calling it a business decision, and Sam's Club has been clearing remaining Series S units at prices reported between $179 and $239 (membership required, stock varies by store). Destructoid reported similar pullbacks among Australian and Oceania retailers.

The pattern worth internalising: list price is going up, but the number of retailers willing to sell below it is also going up, for the opposite reason. That's an unusual situation, and it's the single biggest lever on what you pay.

## The 512GB vs 1TB Question, and Where the Storage Actually Goes

The Series S ships as digital-only, so everything you own lives on its internal drive. That makes storage the decision that matters most, and 512GB doesn't mean 512GB of games.

Owners on Amazon's listing have been blunt about this for years. One long-term reviewer noted that roughly 350GB ends up usable after system overhead, which is why they couldn't keep Microsoft Flight Simulator and Forza Horizon 5 installed at the same time. Independent testing puts the figure around 364GB free.

Here's why that hurts more than it looks:

- Modern releases routinely weigh 80–150GB after patches.
- Storage expansion runs through Microsoft's proprietary Seagate cards, not a cheap off-the-shelf NVMe drive.
- A standard USB drive works only for last-generation titles, not Series X|S games.

The 1TB model exists precisely for this problem, and in most markets it costs meaningfully less than buying a 512GB console plus an expansion card. If you plan to keep more than a handful of current-gen games installed, do that maths before checkout rather than after.

## What You Give Up Versus the Series X

The two consoles run the same dashboard and the same library. The difference is what happens under the hood.

| Spec | Xbox Series S | Xbox Series X |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GPU | 4 TFLOPS, 20 CUs @ 1.565GHz | 12 TFLOPS, 52 CUs @ 1.825GHz |
| CPU | 8-core Zen 2 @ 3.6GHz | 8-core Zen 2 @ 3.8GHz |
| Memory | 10GB GDDR6 | 16GB GDDR6 |
| Storage | 512GB or 1TB NVMe SSD | 1TB (2TB on some models) |
| Disc drive | None | 4K UHD Blu-ray (not on Digital Edition) |
| Target | Up to 1440p @ 120fps | Up to 4K @ 60fps |
| 8K output | No | Yes |

Two practical consequences. First, the Series S mostly renders at 1440p and falls back to 1080p on demanding titles — fine on a monitor or a mid-size TV, visibly softer on a large 4K panel. Second, no disc drive means no second-hand game market, no borrowed discs, and no bargain bin. Every purchase goes through the digital store or a subscription.

TechSpot's aggregated metascore sits at 80 from 19 expert reviews. Reviewers consistently praised the size, the near-silent operation, and the Game Pass value, and consistently criticised the expansion storage cost, the missing Wi-Fi 6, and the lack of native 4K. The same aggregator shows a 9.6 user score from over 31,000 ratings — worth knowing, though single-number user scores flatter popular hardware, so don't read too much into it.

## Who It Still Makes Sense For

GameSpot's argument that the Series S went "from no-brainer to no thank you" is fair on price alone. An under-$300 entry point with Game Pass attached was a genuinely easy recommendation. A $500 one is not, and the plugging of the value gap runs deeper than the console: Game Pass tiers have been rewritten repeatedly, day-one releases moved behind higher tiers, and the Ultimate tier jumped from $20 to $30 a month at one point before settling lower again — GameSpot reported the lineup at $10 (Essential), $15 (Premium) and $23 (Ultimate) by May 2026.

Still, there are cases where it's the right box:

- A secondary console for a bedroom, dorm, or office, where 1440p is the ceiling anyway.
- Households with a 1080p or 1440p TV that aren't being replaced soon.
- Anyone who treats it as a Game Pass machine and rarely buys games outright.
- Travel — it's small and light enough to move between locations without ceremony.

And there are clear cases where it isn't: a large 4K living-room display, a desire to buy and resell discs, or a plan to keep a dozen current-gen titles installed at once. In those situations the extra spend on a Series X is doing real work, not just buying prestige.

## The Real Total Is Not the Sticker Price

Budget for the ecosystem, not just the box. A reasonable three-year estimate for a Series S buyer:

- Console: whatever you actually pay after retailer discounts
- Game Pass (Premium tier): $15/month, so $180 a year
- One or two full-price digital games: $70 each
- Possibly an expansion card: often $150+

That's how a $300 console turns into a $900 commitment. Which is exactly why the buying channel matters more than the console spec sheet.

## Quick Answers

**Is the Xbox Series S still worth buying?**
At retailer discount prices, as a second console or a Game Pass machine on a 1080p/1440p screen, yes. At full list price against a Series X, the value case has genuinely weakened — that's not marketing, it's arithmetic.

**Can I get cashback on a console purchase?**
Sometimes. It depends entirely on the individual retailer's exclusion list on ShopBack. Check the store page, not the platform-wide homepage.

**When is the best time to buy?**
Sale windows and retailer stock-clearing events. Costco and Sam's Club pulling Xbox hardware created genuinely unusual discounts; similar situations repeat when a retailer exits a product line. Watch the 1st of each month too, since that's when Plus Day rates spike.

**512GB or 1TB?**
If you keep more than three or four current-gen games installed, the 1TB model usually costs less than the console-plus-expansion-card route.

**Will cashback cover the price increases?**
No. A few percent back does not offset a $200 increase. It softens the deal, it doesn't rescue it.
